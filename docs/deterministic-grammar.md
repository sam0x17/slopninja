Materialize edits with explicit rules over text spans and grammatical annotations. A rule licenses a candidate string; it does not prove semantic equivalence. Keep the generated text, observed feature change, rule evidence and preservation judgment separate. This path needs no LLM generation or detector calls. The current dependency analysis still uses a trained parser, whose errors must remain visible.

The initial catalog should favor narrow, auditable operations. The restrictions below intentionally sacrifice some valid edits. They describe recommended guards and test expectations, not a claim that an implementation already enforces every guard.

| Operation | Minimum preconditions | Candidate and remaining risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Negative-auxiliary contraction | A fixed spelling map; an auxiliary and its own attached `not`; an overt preceding subject in the same clause, or a separately recognized `do` imperative. No inversion, quotation, code, explicit emphasis or correlative `not only`. | `The service does not stop.` → `The service doesn't stop.` Preserve tense and negation. Formality and emphasis may change. |
| Negative-auxiliary expansion | A fixed unambiguous negative form in a licensed declarative or imperative position. Exclude ambiguous `'s`, `'d`, `ain't`, tags and negative questions. Handle `cannot` ↔ `can't`, never spaced `can not` automatically. | `The service doesn't stop.` → `The service does not stop.` Expansion in a question can require moving `not`, so a literal replacement is insufficient. |
| Present passive-relative reduction | Restrictive subject-relative attached to a nominal; overt `that/which` as passive subject; exactly finite present `is/are` plus a verbal past participle. No other auxiliary, modal, negation, progressive/perfect chain, comma-delimited nonrestrictive relative, or uncertain attachment. | `Files that are stored locally remain available.` → `Files stored locally remain available.` Remove only the relative pronoun and finite auxiliary. Preserve complements and modifiers. Finite present marking disappears, so temporal interpretation still needs review. No guessed inverse. |
| Independent coordination split | Two top-level finite predicates with their own overt subjects; explicit `and/but`; contiguous clause spans. Reject embedded reports, conditions, shared auxiliaries/modifiers, negation, modality, correlatives, ellipsis and nested coordination. | `The mirror stays online, and users keep working.` → `The mirror stays online. And users keep working.` Retain the connective and its relation; do not silently turn contrast into mere succession. |
| Semicolon split | A real punctuation boundary between complete clauses; the configured parse licenses root-like `parataxis/conj`, with separate subjects. Reject list punctuation, parentheticals, quotes, fragments and subordinate/reporting scope. | `A; B.` → `A. B.` Keep any `however/therefore` marker already present. An ambiguous or `ccomp` parse is skipped by the initial strict rule. |
| Sentence join | Adjacent complete declaratives in the same paragraph, with no heading, list, quotation, question, fragment, condition, modal or negation scope to merge. Safe capitalization must be established. | `A. B.` → `A; b.` Use punctuation, not invented `because/therefore` links. Preserve `I`, proper names, acronyms and proper adjectives. A closer rhetorical association remains a possible tonal change. |

Contraction spelling alone does not establish interchangeability in context. Cambridge distinguishes ambiguous `'s`/`'d`, and describes contractions as register-sensitive. Its account of negation places uncontracted `not` after the subject in negative questions and notes the stronger, more formal force of `Do not` imperatives. [Contractions](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/contractions), [negation and negative questions](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/uk/grammar/british-grammar/not_2).

The relevant dependency conventions also have limits. UD explicitly says its basic coordination representation cannot distinguish all shared dependents from first-conjunct-only dependents; a `conj` edge is not an independence certificate. [UD coordination](https://universaldependencies.org/u/dep/conj.html). English finite relative modifiers use `acl:relcl`, while nonfinite counterparts can use `acl`; that label change describes grammar, not preservation. [UD relative clauses](https://universaldependencies.org/u/dep/acl-relcl.html). `parataxis` also covers parentheticals, reported speech, bylines and tags, so it alone cannot authorize a semicolon edit. [UD parataxis](https://universaldependencies.org/u/dep/parataxis.html).

Use these minimal pairs as rejection and diagnostic cases. All example sentences here are synthetic.

| Case | Unsafe operation or misleading inference | Required result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Don't you agree?` | Literal expansion gives `Do not you agree?` | Reject inversion; moving `not` would need a separate rule. |
| `Aren't I eligible?` | Literal expansion gives `Are not I eligible?` | Reject; the idiomatic contracted form is not a simple agreement-preserving substitution. |
| `You can not only read but also write.` | Contracting adjacent `can not` changes the correlative construction. | Reject spaced `can not`, including this case. |
| `She's finished.` / `I'd read it.` | The short forms do not uniquely choose `is/has` or `had/would`. | Reject ambiguous suffixes in this catalog. |
| `She is taller than I am.` | Positive contraction would leave `than I'm`. | Positive contractions are outside the initial catalog. |
| `DO NOT restart the server.` | Contracting discards overt emphasis. | Preserve emphatic spans; ordinary imperatives still require tone review. |
| `Files that store records...` / `Files that users store...` | Subject-active and object relatives are not passive subject-relatives. | Reject reduction, even if a parser returns some relative-clause label. |
| `Files that were stored...` / `Files that have been stored...` / `Files that are being stored...` | Removing the auxiliary chain loses tense or aspect information. | Reject all three under the present-simple rule. |
| `Files that are not stored...` / `Files that may be stored...` | Negation and possibility constrain membership in the described set. | Reject reduction under this catalog. |
| `Files that are easy to read...` | An adjectival/copular predicate is not the required passive. | Reject; do not guess a participial paraphrase. |
| `The files, which are stored locally, remain available.` | Removing commas changes restrictive versus supplementary information. | Skip nonrestrictive relatives; never drop their delimiters. |
| `If the disk fails, the mirror takes over, and users keep working.` | Two subjects do not make the second result unconditional. | Reject shared conditional scope. |
| `We believe that the mirror works and the logs are complete.` | Splitting can turn attributed belief into the narrator's assertion. | Reject embedded coordination. |
| `The service reads and writes records.` / `Maya reads logs, and Lee reports.` | Shared material, missing subjects or lexical ambiguity can defeat a clause template. | Require positive evidence of two independent clauses; skip uncertain parses. |
| `Either the disk failed or the controller failed.` / `Not only did it stop, but it lost data.` | Dropping or relocating connectives changes alternatives or focus. | Reject correlatives, inversion and unsupported connective types. |
| `The sites are London, England; Paris, France; and Boston, Massachusetts.` | Semicolons delimit list items, not finite clauses. | Reject splitting. |
| `The cache is warm. Polish engineers maintain it.` | Lowercasing `Polish` changes meaning; `ADJ` is not a sufficient case guard. | Reject unsafe decapitalization. Likewise protect `I`, `May` and `The Hague`. |
| `The cache is warm.` followed by a new paragraph | Joining crosses an intentional discourse boundary. | Reject cross-paragraph joins. |

Parser checks performed on 2026-09-08 used local `spaCy 3.8.16` with `en_core_web_sm 3.8.0`, without generation or detector calls. Fifteen synthetic cases covered the forms above. The intended simple passive had `that/nsubjpass`, `are/auxpass`, and `stored/relcl/VBN`. Crucially, `stored` carried `Tense=Past|Aspect=Perf|VerbForm=Part` while finite `are` carried present tense. Determine finite tense/aspect from the auxiliary construction, not the participle's morphological tag in isolation.

The same probe correctly exposed an `advcl` conditional on the first coordination head, but it also produced imperfect parses: the ordinary semicolon example had a `ccomp` first clause, and the active-relative example did not receive its intended analysis. These are observed parser limitations, not failed operator tests. The strict catalog should skip uncertain cases rather than repair them from a dependency label. Pin the actual label scheme: the current English spaCy model uses labels such as `relcl`, `auxpass` and `nsubjpass`, which are not verbatim UD v2 labels. Any normalization needs a versioned mapping. [spaCy linguistic annotations](https://spacy.io/usage/linguistic-features#dependency-parse).

The fresh workspace implements paths, full ordered frames and morphological bundles. Its exact definitions are in [the workspace guide](../grammar/README.md). The following alternatives are research proposals with different denominators or truncation choices; they must receive distinct schema identities if implemented:

| Family | Suggested coordinate | Denominator and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Directed dependency paths | Two- and three-edge parent paths encoding UPOS, relation and direction; exclude lexical content initially. | Eligible paths of that exact length within a sentence, excluding punctuation and artificial-root crossings. Keep lengths as separate families. |
| Head frames | Head UPOS plus an ordered or unordered multiset of immediate `(relation, child UPOS)` slots, with repeated-slot counts capped at `2+`. | Eligible nonpunctuation heads with at least one eligible child. Declare ordering and capping in the schema; do not mix them. |
| Morphological bundles | Observed `VerbForm/Tense/Aspect/Voice/Mood` bundles, separately for verbs and auxiliaries; pronoun `Person/Number/Case` bundles. | Eligible tokens of the declared POS class. Preserve missing-feature coverage explicitly; absence is not automatically a negative value. |
| Auxiliary constructions | Predicate-local auxiliary lemma/order, finite-auxiliary morphology and passive evidence. | Eligible predicate constructions under a versioned head definition. Count a construction once, not once per auxiliary. These are grammatical forms, not certainty labels. |
| Clause relations | Matrix-to-relative/complement/adjunct/conjunct patterns, with overt-subject and connective indicators. | Eligible clause attachments for a categorical distribution; alternatively binary sentence incidence / parsed sentences. Multiple occurrences per sentence require a count metric, not a bounded rate. |
| Sibling combinations | Selected pairs of dependency slots sharing a head, such as subject–object or subject–complement. | Eligible same-head slot pairs, not all token pairs in the document. Specify duplicate-slot handling and surface order. |

Validation should establish what the engine actually does:

1. Freeze positive and negative fixtures before tuning rules. Test candidate presence/absence, exact old/new spans and guard reasons. Include short clauses, multiline prose, Unicode apostrophes, decomposed accents, abbreviations, quoted examples, lists and punctuation inside parentheses.
2. Apply edits to original UTF-8 byte spans. The earlier profiling bridge normalizes Unicode before parsing; those offsets must not be reused against original bytes without a mapping. The fresh grammar adapter parses unchanged text and converts character offsets to verified byte spans. Verify every old span exactly, reject overlap, and prove all untouched bytes stay unchanged.
3. Reparse every materialized candidate and measure its full feature vector. Check expected structural changes, but permit a rule to return no candidate if the configured parser cannot establish its preconditions. Preserve signed numbers, entities, references and protected text as preliminary checks.
4. Test repeatability and graph behavior: stable enumeration, finite depth/branch budgets, content-hash deduplication, no contraction/expansion cycles consuming the search budget, and reparsing after each parent edit instead of reusing stale offsets. Keep all provenance edges when distinct rule paths reach the same text.
5. Evaluate preservation separately on complete source/candidate pairs, including tense, modality, attribution, conditions, quantifiers, argument relations and emphasis. Record reviewer type and exact hashes; a syntactic guard or improved vector distance must never set a semantic-equivalence flag automatically.

Every candidate record should identify its parent/source hash, exact resulting hash, rule and catalog versions, edited spans, parse identity, passed guards, unsupported or uncertain conditions, and observed vector delta. A deterministic search can make the possibilities concrete and reproducible while leaving unsupported rewrites ungenerated and semantic acceptance unresolved.

Miners can eventually propose both new grammar operators and new feature spaces. Each proposal should freeze an executable artifact hash, dependency/parser identity, operator catalog, feature definitions and denominators, reference-data manifest, fitted parameters, distance function, and search policy/budget. Give the extractor and space separate content-addressed identities: changing normalization, a feature dictionary, weighting or reference fitting creates a new identity, even if the displayed coordinate names remain the same.

Evaluate proposals on validator-owned held-out editing tasks, with the same inputs and resource limits. Measure claim fidelity, readability for the intended audience, useful accepted edits, failure rate and computational cost independently of the submitted distance function. A miner cannot demonstrate improvement merely by inventing a space in which its own outputs are close to the target; distances from different spaces are not interchangeable scores. Retain the unchanged-source baseline, and use operator-only and representation-only comparisons when attributing a gain. Keep evaluation families hidden from fitting, threshold selection and repeated adaptation; development feedback belongs to a separate split.

Freeze the evaluation rubric and exact candidate hashes before review. Distinguish human judgments, assistant judgments and mechanical checks; no submitted feature metric may certify its own meaning preservation. Private pre-2025 correspondence remains local to the authorized evaluator: do not publish its text, excerpts, identifiable metadata or text-bearing parser caches with a proposal. Use synthetic fixtures in public documentation and expose only the permitted aggregate evaluation results.
