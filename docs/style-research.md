Represent an author's style as a distribution of measurable choices within a register. Keep author resemblance, rhetorical presentation, audience accessibility and meaning preservation separate. The design below is a proposal for this project, informed by five research papers and the official Universal Dependencies documentation; it is not a validated detector-evasion method. Sources checked 2026-09-08.

Function-word frequencies are useful stylistic evidence, but even pronouns can vary with topic, genre and narrative perspective. They are not a content-free author fingerprint. [Kestemont, *Function Words in Authorship Attribution* (2014)](https://aclanthology.org/W14-0908.pdf) explains these limitations. In attribution experiments, masking less frequent words improved performance under cross-topic conditions; the benefit depended on the corpus, and the paper did not establish equivalent protection against genre differences. [Stamatatos, *Authorship Attribution Using Text Distortion* (2017)](https://aclanthology.org/E17-1107.pdf).

Style change can conflict with content preservation and naturalness. Mir and colleagues measured all three separately and observed tradeoffs in their sentiment-transfer experiments. Those results motivate separate evaluations here; their Yelp-based metrics are not established tests of philosophical fidelity or author imitation. [Mir et al., *Evaluating Style Transfer for Text* (2019)](https://aclanthology.org/N19-1049.pdf).

Controllable simplification provides a useful starting point for audience controls: length, paraphrasing, lexical difficulty and syntactic difficulty. ACCESS also demonstrated interaction between controls: reducing other complexity measures often shortened text unless length was constrained, and extreme settings could produce poor output. This supports measuring achieved changes instead of treating slider settings as guarantees. [Martin et al., *Controllable Sentence Simplification* (2020)](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.577.pdf).

The following table proposes a future UD-based extraction contract. The current prototype uses the existing Rust/spaCy extractors and their versioned denominators; [the implementation guide](style-space.md) describes that narrower feature set. For the proposed contract, let `N` be the number of integer-ID UD word rows with `UPOS != PUNCT`, `S` the number of sentences, and `P` the number of nonempty prose paragraphs. Keep raw counts and denominators alongside every rate. Store missing or zero-denominator measurements as unavailable, not zero. Exclude CoNLL-U multiword-token range rows and empty nodes from word counts; they are distinct from the ordinary word rows. Preserve their raw representation for audit. [Official CoNLL-U format](https://universaldependencies.org/format.html).

Pin the prose renderer, tokenizer, parser/model, UD version, dictionaries and feature schema. Preserve paragraph boundaries and source offsets. Exclude markup, code, equations and bibliography through explicit rules; preserve references and equations separately for fidelity checks. Report excluded material and parser coverage. Compare like-sized prose windows as well as complete documents, with their actual lengths retained.

| Feature family | Proposed measurements and units | Interpretation and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lexical habits | Lowercased surface-form and lemma counts per 1,000 `N`; case patterns separately; document prevalence as documents containing a form / eligible documents. | Keep topic vocabulary separate from function words. Technical terms belong in the protected claim inventory, even if statistically unusual. |
| Function words | Versioned closed-class inventory counts per 1,000 `N`; pronoun person/number, articles, auxiliaries, prepositions and conjunctions as separate groups. | Preserve individual forms as well as groups. Negation and modal auxiliaries also require semantic review. |
| Word sequences | Word/POS bigrams and trigrams divided by eligible within-sentence windows of that order; boundary-sensitive character n-grams divided by character windows. | Never join across sentence boundaries accidentally. A masked-content channel can test topic sensitivity; keep the readable text intact. |
| POS and dependency patterns | UPOS rates per 1,000 `N`; `(dependent UPOS, relation, head UPOS)` counts per 1,000 basic arcs with nonpunctuation endpoints and a nonzero head. | Retain relation subtypes as well as a separately derived base relation. Basic and enhanced dependency graphs must not be pooled. [UD relation inventory](https://universaldependencies.org/u/dep/). |
| Clausal constructions | Counts of `acl:relcl`, `advcl`, `ccomp`, `xcomp` and `csubj` families per 100 `S`; distinguish relative clauses from other noun-modifying clauses. | These are labeled construction counts, not a complete census of clauses. `xcomp` and `ccomp` differ in subject interpretation, not simply sentence length. [Relative clauses](https://universaldependencies.org/u/dep/acl-relcl.html), [open complements](https://universaldependencies.org/u/dep/xcomp.html), [clausal complements](https://universaldependencies.org/u/dep/ccomp.html). |
| Subject and voice patterns | Pronoun-headed nominal subjects / all `nsubj`-family arcs; expletives per 100 `S`; distinct heads with `nsubj:pass`, `csubj:pass` or `aux:pass` evidence per 100 `S`. | Count each passive head once. A syntactic subject is not necessarily an agent, and its head need not be a verb. [UD `nsubj`](https://universaldependencies.org/u/dep/nsubj.html), [passive subjects](https://universaldependencies.org/u/dep/nsubj-pass.html). |
| Subject semantics | Human, collective, other organism, artifact, abstract, expletive and unknown labels; each count / subject occurrences, with unknown coverage shown. | Proposed semantic labels require contextual annotation. Do not infer them from capitalization, `NOUN`, or a pronoun list. UD's animacy feature is not a universal English semantic classifier. [UD animacy](https://universaldependencies.org/u/feat/Animacy.html). |
| Syntactic load | Maximum dependency depth per sentence; mean and upper quantiles of arc distance in nonpunctuation word positions; both stratified by sentence length. | Define root depth as zero and omit punctuation nodes from these summaries. Parser errors and sentence length can alter the result. These are proxies, not direct comprehension measures. |
| Rhythm and layout | Words/sentence and sentences/paragraph distributions: median, quartiles, upper tail and dispersion; mean absolute difference between adjacent sentence lengths; punctuation events per 1,000 `N`. | Retain distributions. Two texts with the same mean can have different pacing. Quotation, question-mark and parenthesis counts describe form; they do not identify rhetorical intent. |
| Discourse and repetition | Versioned connective matches per 100 `S`, sentence-initial matches per 100 `S`, repeated opening patterns / eligible adjacent-sentence pairs. | Distinguish observed strings from annotated concession, contrast, explanation and conclusion relations. Moving a connective can change the argument. |
| Lexical accessibility | Sentence-level upper-quartile log frequency-rank summaries from a pinned reference lexicon; lookup coverage and protected-term coverage reported separately. | Frequency is a difficulty proxy. Audience knowledge of the topic needs separate evidence; an uncommon but defined technical term need not be replaced. |

For the inanimate-subject question, measure the combination of subject meaning and predicate use. “The rock fell” and “The paper argues” should not receive the same editorial diagnosis merely because both subjects are inanimate. Decide whether the construction fits the author and context using labeled examples. An instruction such as “Press Enter to open” can use an understood reader as actor, but automatically inventing a person behind every abstract subject would change some claims.

Store author profiles by `author x language x register`, with topic, date, work, edition, editor/translator and provenance as metadata. Hold out complete works or source families before generating windows. Revisions, quotations, shared prompts and derived texts stay with their source family. Evaluate both held-out works in a familiar register and explicitly separate cross-topic or cross-register conditions. The existing small scientific pilot can test plumbing; it cannot establish an individual author's style across several kinds of writing.

For each stratum, retain document-level feature vectors, means, quantiles and uncertainty from resampling independent source groups. Give documents or works explicit weights so a long book does not silently dominate. Start with standardized feature groups and diagonal or regularized distances; a full covariance estimate can be unstable with few independent works. Compare a candidate's distance with held-out same-author distances, rather than forcing every paragraph onto the author's average. These are proposed statistical controls, not confidence guarantees. Learn feature selection, normalization and weights on training material only. A two-dimensional visualization is a projection, not evidence that style occupies one unique point.

The proposed implementation fits this separation: author/register-scoped JSONL profiles, equal source-group weighting and separate feature-family distances make the comparison inspectable. Measuring the open-class lexicon while giving it zero default ranking weight is a useful initial defense against topic matching; function words and syntax still need cross-topic evaluation. Label initial length/lexical accessibility presets as heuristics, and report both requested and achieved measurements. Keep rhetorical strength as a qualitative editing-and-review instruction until paired examples support a calibrated direction. A hash-bound meaning review records a judgment about one candidate; it is not an automatic meaning certificate.

| Requested control | Editable presentation | Required boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Author resemblance | Choices among supported wordings, sentence patterns, pacing and discourse habits in the relevant register. | Preserve claims and avoid copying identifying phrases from reference works as a shortcut. A closer vector is not proof of authorship. |
| Rhetorical strength | Placement and emphasis of an existing claim; directness of presentation; reduced repetition when its argumentative function survives. | Preserve epistemic strength, attribution, quantifiers, negation, conditions and logical direction. Neither strengthening nor weakening an assertion is a neutral style edit. |
| Audience accessibility | Lower syntactic load, clearer antecedents, familiar nontechnical vocabulary, and source-supported explanations of protected terms. | A novice-oriented version must retain qualifications and argument steps. Length is a soft target; added explanation must be identified and supported. |
| Edit extent | Target/source length ratio and a versioned edit-distance measure, reported as achieved values. | More editing is not automatically better. A no-change candidate remains eligible when alternatives fail preservation. |

Hedges and modals require scope-aware protection. “May cause,” “does cause,” “cannot cause” and “must cause” differ in meaning; frequency matching must not exchange them freely. BioScope annotates both uncertainty/negation cues and their scopes, with contextual ambiguity and adjudication, rather than relying only on a cue list. Its biomedical annotations motivate the safeguard but do not validate a philosophical rhetorical-strength classifier. The article's open license is also distinct from the corpus's stated research/business terms; check dataset rights before importing examples. [Vincze et al., *The BioScope corpus* (2008)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2586758/).

Do not implement an automatic hedge-removal knob. A lower hedge count can reflect stronger assertions, deleted uncertain claims or a different topic, rather than more effective rhetoric. Conversely, adding hedges to a confident identity claim weakens the author's position. Review the cue, its scope and its evidential role together; adjust presentation around that protected meaning.

The next labeled set should contain source/revision pairs with claim-span alignment, entity/number retention, modality and negation scope, quantifier scope, attribution, conditional direction, unsupported additions, and preserved or changed rhetorical force. Add intended-audience labels, reader comprehension judgments and examples of successful terminology explanation. For subject semantics and discourse relations, collect contextual labels and adjudicated disagreements. Author resemblance needs judgments across multiple works; accessibility needs judgments from the intended audience. Keep assistant assessments explicitly separate from human labels.

Use a bounded edit search: freeze the source, claim inventory, author references, controls and proposal budget; generate candidates first for individual lexical choices, then sentence structure, then paragraph organization. Record achieved feature changes and claim-aligned revisions. Reject meaning changes before comparing readability or style fit. Rank surviving candidates on separate axes instead of hiding a preservation failure inside a weighted average. Freeze the selected process before testing new source families, and report failures as well as wins.

Detector response remains a separate empirical outcome. None of these sources establishes that approaching an author's feature distribution guarantees a low Pangram score. Any such relationship must be measured on new texts with detector/version records and the same fidelity gate; the author-style distance itself is not evidence that the detector objective was met.
